# Tags and links

#getting started#

You want to keep projects, clients or topics apart without sorting notes into folders. Give each one a tag, and connect the notes with links. This note shows how.

## Give a project a tag

Type `#` and a word anywhere in a note, like `#garden`, and the note has that tag. Every tag shows up in the sidebar. Tap or click one to see its notes.

- A slash nests a tag inside another: `#work/clients/acme`. The sidebar shows a tree, and a parent tag also covers everything below it
- A tag with spaces ends with another hash: `#book club#`
- Typing `#` suggests the tags you already use
- On the Mac, drag a note onto a tag in the sidebar to give it that tag
- To rename a tag, change its icon or pin it, right-click it on the Mac, or touch and hold it on iPhone and iPad and choose Edit Tag. Renaming a tag updates every note that uses it

Look at the sidebar: this note has the tag `getting started`, and so do the other notes of this tour.

## Link notes together

Put a title between double square brackets to link to another note. `[[Welcome to Theo Notes]]` becomes this link: [[Welcome to Theo Notes]]. On the Mac, hold ⌘ and click a link to open its note, or hold ⌥⌘ and click to open it in a new window. On iPhone and iPad, tap the link.

- Type `[[` and pick a note from the suggestions
- Add a slash to link to a heading inside a note: `[[Write and format/Lists and to-dos]]`
- Add a bar to show other words: `[[Find anything|how to search]]` shows as [[Find anything|how to search]]
- Leave the title out to link to a heading in the same note: `[[/Give a project a tag]]`

## See who links here

On the Mac, open the Info panel with the ⓘ button in the toolbar and choose Backlinks. On iPhone and iPad, tap Note Actions and choose Note Info. It lists the notes that link here, and the notes that mention this title without a link yet, with a button that turns the mention into a link.

If you change the title of a note, the links that point to it are updated for you.
